# Release notes

## 1.0.0

- A **crash in the goggles** with the drone's view in the main view is fixed (owner; P144 in BUGS.txt).
- The **target's box, label and brackets** sit on the target with Dynamic helmet too (owner): they were drawn about ten rows too low, so a small far target was on the box's upper edge. The **drone's brackets** are on the drone in widescreen (they sat up and left of it). P143 in BUGS.txt.
- **Flying the drone** (Ctrl and the movement keys): at 60 frames a second it turned 2.4 times too fast, because the game turns an aircraft once a frame; it now turns as at the game's 25. And it turns while a forward or back key is held, and a strafe key with a forward or back key flies it diagonally: the game used only one of two held keys (owner; a 1996 bug, G47 in BUGS.txt).
- **Reflecting water** at the very top of the picture read one byte before the picture's memory (a 1996 bug, harmless; found by the memory checker with the drone's view in the goggles; G48 in BUGS.txt).
- A rare bad memory read while drawing the sky beside a tall canyon wall (seen once, in a Random Scenario with the 360 view; P145 in BUGS.txt).
- **Gimbal mode stays on** through the options screen, the help screen (H) and the game's own options screen; opening one of them switched it off (owner). A new mission still starts with it off.
- **Dragging the full map** with the right mouse button moves it the game's own way again with the Advanced controls too (owner): the Advanced controls had turned the direction round.
- **The keys to know first** show for new players: the first-start page says "In a mission: H = help and controls, O = options and presets", and for the first seconds of a mission white lines on the view say H: HELP, O: OPTIONS and, with the Advanced controls, F: ALIGN BODY TO HEADVIEW, X: GIMBAL MODE and SHIFT (HOLD): FREE MOUSE CURSOR. The line stops coming once the help screen has been opened (`help_seen=on` in `tnfree.ini`), and the Classic preset never shows it.
- A **tool tip** showing while a key switches the cockpit's resolution is taken away with the old screen; the game went on copying its old rectangle, which at 320 x 200 lies outside the screen (a 1996 bug, G46 in BUGS.txt).
- Freefall **finds an installed game by itself** at the first start: a Steam copy (in any Steam library folder) or a GOG copy. The folder window only asks when it finds none. Both layouts are known: Steam's (the game in `TNOVA`, the CD image in `_ISO`) and GOG's (the game in `TN`, the CD in `CD`); the CD's files are also found as a plain folder next to the game's. It only reads (the registry and Steam's list of library folders) and writes nothing.
- Freefall comes with the **GeneralUser GS** SoundFont (version 2.0.3, by S. Christian Collins) in the **Music** folder: Music: SoundFont (AWE32) plays it unless you choose another SoundFont. Its licence is beside it (`GeneralUser-GS-LICENSE.txt`) and in THIRD-PARTY.txt.
- Freefall comes with the **Freefall Music** pack in the Music folder (`Freefall-MP3.zip`): the game's music recorded as MP3 by Rich "Weeds" Nagel (http://www.richnagel.net), shared with his permission. The **Freefall preset** plays it (Music pack: Freefall-MP3); **Vanilla+** and **Classic** set Music pack to Off, so the game's own music plays as the Music line sets it. Choosing another pack makes the preset Custom. The READMEs call the options page Music & Movies now.
- Freefall comes with the **Freefall Mod Pack 1.2** in the **Mods** folder (`Freefall.zip`): the 16:9 cockpits and goggles of every suit with Dynamic helmet, the suits' arms, the new explosions, deaths, smoke and craters, and the trees, bushes and rocks. The **Freefall preset** uses it, as before; it is no longer a separate download.
- **Credits** page: the title is white and in capitals (TERRA NOVA: FREEFALL), Created By reads Nikolay "Cyberion" Ivliev, and a new line credits the MP3 music: MP3 Music Synthesized By Rich "Weeds" Nagel.
- The options screen's **pictures are built into `tnfree.exe`**: the release has no Previews folder any more. A Previews folder next to `tnfree.exe` still goes over them (for trying new stills).
- **Options screen, owner's round**: the help line now describes the setting and the value **under the pointer** as you move over it (before, mostly the chosen value's help showed). **Gameplay**: Stereo sound moved under Invert mouse; the right column has the game's own **Difficulty level** (Neighborhood Watch to World War; grey in a mission, as on the game's screen), then Mission select and the game's **Tool tips**. **Sound & Movies** is now **Music & Movies**: Music pack under SoundFont for AWE32, and the game's **Suit speech**, **Dropship speech** (Voice and Text, each on or off) and **Briefings** (Video or Text) under Movie size. **Mods**: Cockpit, Arms, Effects and Objects one under the other, now called **Cockpit mod**, **Arms mod**, **Effects mod** and **Objects mod** (Music pack keeps its name); Dynamic helmet on the right. New pictures (owner's screenshots) for **Auto target camera**, **Head view cone**, **Map smoothing** and **Radar without MFD** (On). Show enemies without MFD's help is now "Show enemies even when MFD screen in Goggles is off".
- **O opens the Freefall options screen** now and **Alt+O the game's own options screen** in a mission (owner's choice; it was the other way round). O is still a letter while you type a saved game's name, and on the game's own options screen O is still its Accept. Esc, O or CLOSE closes the Freefall screen.
- The options screen **sounds like the game's own**: a button click plays the game's options click, CLOSE and the boxes the game's Accept sound, the **Stereo sound** buttons the game's "left, right" test, and the **FX** and **Speech volume** sliders a sound at the new level, as on the game's screen. The first-start question takes a click **anywhere on a card** (not only PLAY), with the sound of the outfitting's Review Video Briefing.
- **Switch all filters off** is gone from the Display page (each filter is still set on its own). **Tree explosion** has pictures now (owner's screenshots), Classic and Realistic.
- **Options screen, owner's round**: the text of the button under the pointer turns orange to show what a click will do (no title lights up under the pointer any more); the chosen value under the pointer shows its text in the panels' grey on orange, as the game's own arrow buttons. **Difficulty level**'s title is red. The Stereo sound test plays "left" then "right" every time (it played "left" once and then nothing until another click), and the FX and Speech volume play their sample at every new level (only the first time before). Alt+O opens the game's own options screen in the menus and the base too, and works with a real Alt key (the game took Alt+O, not its O, and opened nothing); GAME OPTIONS works there too. The game's own options screen has its **FREEFALL OPTIONS** button in the middle above its Cancel / Accept buttons, drawn as those buttons (it was in the bottom right corner). **Difficulty level** says what each level does (enemy damage 60% to 2x, how well and fast enemies aim and fire, a tougher squad on the two hardest; the game's own tables). Settings without a picture (Enemy distance, Scaling, the filters...) show none instead of the last one. **Key bindings**: the list has the game's arrows (turned up and down) at both ends of its bar, and DEFAULT KEYS is no longer orange. The Mission Selector's Main Menu button is now **Cancel**.
- **Options screen, owner's round**: the options screen is **mouse only** now (no Up / Down / Left / Right / Enter, and no Home on the Key bindings page; Esc or O still closes it, Esc goes back from Key bindings, and a key being bound still takes Backspace to clear it). In a mission the greyed **Difficulty level** keeps its title white on red. **Cockpit & Maps**: Show enemies without MFD is under Head view cone, Map smoothing in the right column above Radar without MFD; Show enemies without MFD has a picture now (owner's screenshot), On.
- **Credits**: the game's credits (main menu, Credits) start with a page of Freefall's own, in the credits' font and colours: Terra Nova: Freefall (in the credits' red), "A modern SDL3 port of Terra Nova: Strike Force Centauri (Looking Glass Technologies, 1996)", Created By (in the credits' tan) Cyberion, and the version at the bottom of the page. A click goes on to the game's credits as before.

Movies play in the map's box, with Stop and Fullscreen under it, smoothed when "Smooth videos" is on. Play Mission starts a new campaign at that mission, as `--mission N` does, and takes you straight to its outfitting (the opening movie, quarters, base and briefing video are passed for you, silently, with the mission's name on a black screen meanwhile); the campaign goes on from there. Progress and records are kept in `Saves\missions.ini`, worked out from your saved games the first time. See MISSION SELECTOR in README.txt.

In the game, with a target locked (T, Y or a right click) and the pointer within 20 pixels of its box, the shot goes to the target itself: ahead of it for beams, lobbed onto it for grenades and missiles. A smaller box lets you shoot beside a locked target; with Off your shots always go where you point. 20 px needs the Classic controls style: with Advanced it shows grey and works as 5 px. A `tnfree.ini` from an earlier build gets 20 px with the Classic controls style, 5 px with Advanced.

While a shot would go to the locked target, the crosshair's bright green middle turns red. A `tnfree.ini` from an earlier build gets it On with the Freefall preset's settings, Off otherwise.

**Scaling** (Alt+O, Display page; Fit unless you change it)

**3D resolution** (Alt+O, Display page; Original until you change it)

Edges of the land, buildings and units are sharp; the game's textures stay as they are. The game itself plays exactly the same: only the picture changes. (Called "3D sharpness" before.)
- The target's label (its name, the distance and "Locked" or "Out of range") is drawn into the sharp picture at 3/4 of the game's size, so it stays sharp and no longer covers so much of the view (from 4x; at 2x in the game's high-detail mode it stays the game's own). The land shows its real squares (on the full map finer than the game's pixels), and the units, paths, circles, nav marks, view cone and nav point are drawn sharp, each at its exact place; the map's text stays the game's letters, drawn bigger. The legend box and the zoom buttons stay as the game draws them.

**Widescreen** (Alt+O, Display page; Off until you change it)

It works with any 3D resolution, Original too. The game plays the same: the AI and everything else still work from the original 4:3 view.
- The reticle stays on its units, and the targets' brackets and labels (name, distance, LOCKED) are drawn whole on their units out at the sides too. The mouse works everywhere: the buttons, and aiming, firing and right-click targeting in the whole view, its wide sides too. Round dials look slightly oval. The game's options screen stays 4:3.
- The maps are not stretched: the full map (M) and the cockpit's squad map keep the right proportions and show more of the land at the sides. Zooming out stops where the map shows the land's whole width, so no black shows beside the land (the farthest zoom fits the land exactly). Clicking at the sides works as on the rest of the map (orders, the nav point).

**Arms pack** (Alt+O, Mods page under Cockpit pack; Off until you choose a pack; test build)
- In the goggles view (never in the cockpit or the drone's view) your suit's arm that holds the selected weapon, from the pictures of a mod's `Arms\<suit>\` folder (a grid of poses for the pointer's places, see `Mods\README.txt`): the right arm for a weapon in the right arm, the same pictures mirrored for the left; a weapon in neither arm shows none. The arm follows the pointer on a spring, moving smoothly between the poses (below the poses it moves down with the pointer); with the Advanced controls' mouselook the pointer stays in the middle and the arm sways behind the turn (a pack can set how far the arm sways and how slowly it follows). A shot kicks the arm back, by the weapon's rate of fire: a slow weapon (the Multipulsar and slower) the whole way, a quick one (the Laser) in small soft pulses with the arm held back while the shots keep coming. Selecting a weapon in the other arm lowers the arm out of the view and raises the other from below (the first arm also comes up from below). In the infrared view (I) the arm shows in the infrared's colours (purple to magenta with Sci-fi, warmer than the land, not outlined). Your shots leave from the arm's muzzle (the game's own points sit beside your body, off the screen). Walking, backing up and strafing on the ground move the arm a little: it dips and swings gently from side to side as you go (the same forward, back or sideways, about 2.6 dips a second at full speed), and lags behind a strafe or a walk forward or back (the faster you go, the more; a pack can set how much and how quick, or turn it off). It draws in the sharp 3D view (any 3D resolution, Original too).

**Mission select** (command line `--mission N`; off without it)
- Start tnfree.exe with `--mission N` and New Game starts the campaign at mission N: its briefing, outfitting and the mission, then the campaign goes on from there. README.txt lists the campaign's missions with their numbers and suits (Operation Ivanhoe, 9, brings the Heavy suit; Operation Cold War, 39, is the pirate mission). It uses a start-mission setting the original game has but never let you set; saved games are not changed.

**Anti-aliasing** (Alt+O, Display page under Scaling; Off until you change it; needs 3D resolution 2x, 4x or 6x)
- FXAA: the stairs along the edges of hills, buildings, units and the horizon in the sharp 3D views are smoothed on your graphics card (Direct3D 12 or Vulkan, like the Effects on the Filters page). Only the 3D is smoothed: the HUD, the text, the target's label in the 3D view, the maps and the cockpit's art stay crisp. Without a card that can do it the setting says "not available". Only the picture changes: the game plays the same.

**Terrain distance** (Alt+O, Distance page; Original until you change it; called Terrain detail in earlier test builds)
- Original (the game's; called Low in earlier test builds), Medium or High: the land is drawn as smooth polygons out to 12, 32 or 48 squares instead of the game's flat far columns, so far hills keep their shape. It works with every 3D resolution, Original too. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same.
- High draws the land farthest out. (The far smoothing that High had in an earlier test build is now its own setting, Terrain smoothing.)

**Terrain smoothing** (Alt+O, Detail page; Off until you turn it on)
- The game fills each far hillside with flickering texture noise in flat blocks with hard edges (stairs along the squares and the distance rings); with Terrain smoothing the far land is textured like the near ground: each square's own texture, fixed to the land so it no longer crawls as you move, shrunk smoothly with the distance, and its light and haze blend from point to point. Far hills and mountains look like a continuation of the land around you, and shores by lakes keep the land's texture on the slopes. The outline of the land stays the game's. It works with every Terrain distance and 3D resolution and is drawn on several of your processor's cores (in my tests about 3 to 5 ms more at 6x). Only the picture changes: the game plays the same. (The first test build blended one colour per point instead, which looked blurry; that is gone.)
- The middle distance too: with Terrain distance Medium and High the game leaves some squares between the near ground and the far land without polygons (they showed as blocky patches about halfway out); those are smoothed now as well, so the land is smooth near, in the middle and far.
- Smooth light on the near ground: the game lights its ground in 16 steps, cut to the step below, so slopes show bands of light. With Terrain smoothing the light runs smoothly across each slope, blended between the steps by a fine dither (about 1 to 2 ms more at 6x in my tests).
- Shores: land and water at far shores are split by the land's height, so the gaps that showed in the land by lakes with Water reflection High are gone.

**Object distance** (Alt+O, Distance page under Terrain distance; Original until you change it)
- The game draws each kind of object only out to its own distance and leaves it out farther away: soldiers at 20 squares, most vehicles at 30, buildings at 40 (a few things 50 to 80), and the trees and rocks only on the near ground (out to 12 to 48 squares, with Terrain distance). In the sharp picture that is far less than the land you see. Far draws your squad and other friendly units, vehicles, buildings and effects twice as far and trees out to 48 squares; Farthest 4 times as far (a building out to 160 squares, past the game's limit of 120) and trees out to 96 squares, over the whole far land. Far objects take the land's haze like the land around them. It works with every 3D resolution, Original too, and costs little (in my tests at 6x in mission 2's forest, with about 1,500 trees in view instead of 380, about half a millisecond more).
- The enemy is not seen any sooner: whatever of theirs can shoot (soldiers, vehicles, aircraft, gun turrets and their shots) is left to Enemy distance below. Their buildings without a weapon are drawn far like the rest.
- Only the picture changes: the game plays the same, and the AI sees what it always saw. Clicking still targets only what the game itself would draw.

**Enemy distance** (Alt+O, Distance page under Object distance; Original until you change it)
- Original draws the enemy's soldiers, vehicles, aircraft and their shots at the game's own distance (soldiers and shots 120 m, most vehicles and aircraft 180 m). Far draws them twice as far. Their gun turrets always stay at the game's distance. It works on its own or with Object distance. Only the picture changes: the AI sees what it always saw, but you may spot enemies sooner.

**Fog** (Alt+O, Distance page under Enemy distance; Off until you change it)
- On: the far land fades smoothly into the colour of the sky at the horizon (the pale haze by day, black under the stars at night), and the land past the fog's end is not drawn at all. It fades out at 240 squares, about the game's own drawing distance. The fade follows a gentle curve: the land stays clear longer (at half the way it is only 3/8 fogged) and thickens faster toward the end. In rain and snow the fog is thicker and nearer (it starts at about half the distance and ends at 7/10 of it). (Earlier test builds had Light and Strong; Strong, a thick fog at 140 squares, was taken out, and an old Light or Strong setting reads as On.) Fog is for the look, not for speed: the far land it leaves out is only a thin strip at the horizon, and fading the land costs about as much (in my tests at 6x: the same time as without fog at Terrain distance Medium, about 1 ms more at High). The land, the water, the near ground, buildings and units all fade; a unit fades at most three quarters, so you can still see it in the fog. The game's own haze stays as it is. It works with every 3D resolution, Original too. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same.
- With Object distance it softens the new edge: with Farthest, far buildings, units and trees come out of the haze instead of appearing all at once.
- In the infrared view (I key) too: the land, the water and the objects fade into the infrared's own haze at the horizon (its colour of the sky's haze) and the land past the fog's end is not drawn, as in the normal view; the infrared no longer shows far land the normal view hides.

**True colour** (Alt+O, Detail page under Terrain smoothing; Off until you change it)
- The game draws in 256 colours, so its sky fades to the horizon in steps, its clouds are blocks of a few colours, and the fog is dotted between 16 levels. With True colour the main 3D view's sky, clouds and fog are worked out in full colour after the game has drawn the view: the clouds are soft (each pixel mixed from the cloud picture around it, with a finer layer of the clouds' own shapes so they don't turn blurry), the sky fades smoothly to the horizon, and the fog mixes each pixel with the fog colour by its exact amount, with no dots. Anything drawn over the sky or the fogged land (units, trees, shots, rain) keeps the game's colours, and so does the rest of the picture.
- On + sun glow (experimental): the sky around the sun is lighter and warmer (a small bright glow and a wide soft one, also when the sun is behind the clouds), and the lower the sun, the wider and more orange the glow along the horizon. With Fog on the fog takes the glow too: lighter and warmer toward the sun, and the land fades into it.
- It works with every 3D resolution, Original too, but not in the infrared view (I key). It costs time: in my tests on a 4-core cloud machine at 6x widescreen about 20 ms more a frame (half for noting the pixels while the view is drawn, half for the full colours on several cores); less at lower 3D resolutions. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same.

**Water reflection** (Alt+O, Detail page under True colour; Low, the game's default, until you change it)
- Medium: the sky is reflected in all the water, near and far, at any angle: looking straight down at a lake you see the clouds above you. In the original only far water near the horizon reflects, and the reflection is gone when you look down. Land still mirrors where it is in the view. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same. The reflection uses only the water's own colours (no green specks along the clouds) and fades smoothly from the horizon down, with no line across the water. The water no longer jitters (the original game shook its reflection every frame), mountains mirror cleanly, the mirrored land fades softly at the top of the view when you look down, and the clouds and waves reach far out toward the horizon.
- High (experimental): everything Medium does, and the land and buildings are mirrored where they really stand: an island's reflection is under the island and a tower beside a lake is mirrored in it, even when the water is close and you look down. Medium mirrors only what is in the picture at the same height above the horizon, so close shores and walls could show the wrong land. Units, vehicles and boats are drawn a second time as seen from under the water, so a soldier wading or standing on the shore shows upside down with the underside you would really see; trees, explosions and splashes are mirrored from their own pictures. Tracers, sparks and laser bolts are not reflected. High costs a little more time each frame than Medium. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same.
- The water is drawn on several of your processor's cores (up to 8), so Medium and High cost about half as much time as before on a 4-core processor.

**Water ripples** (Alt+O, Detail page under Water reflection; Off until you turn it on; needs Water reflection: Medium or High)
- On: moving waves on the water: the reflection sways and the waves catch lighter and darker sky. The waves stay in place on the water as you move (no shaking), run with the mission's wind, and fade out in the distance so far water doesn't flicker. Soldiers standing or walking in the water send out rings that spread and fade (more often while they move), with light and dark crests so they show even on a plain blue lake. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same. (An earlier test build had Calm and Lively; Lively is gone, and both read as On.)

**Water effects** (Alt+O, Detail page under Water ripples; Off until you turn it on; needs Water reflection: Medium or High)
- Splashes: where a shot, a shell or a missile hits the water, and where a soldier or a body falls into it: white foam spreading from the middle, a spout of water and drops thrown up and falling back, and two rings. Bigger weapons make bigger splashes.
- Wakes: rings left behind vehicles moving on the water.
- Shallows: near the shore the water is a clearly lighter blue, a square wide near you and widening to about 3 squares far off (from the water's own colours, so it blends with the rest of the lake), fading out toward the open water and in the distance. A small lake or pond, where all the water is near the shore, gets less of it: full only where the water reaches 4 squares from the shore, none where it reaches only one. (Earlier test builds made it greener and as strong in a pond as by a sea, then too faint: 2 squares wide and only half as light, then 3 squares wide near you too, which looked like a halo around shores, piers and boats.)
- Only the picture changes: the game plays the same (the effects use none of the game's random numbers).

**Map smoothing** (Alt+O, Cockpit & maps page under Map view cone; Off, the game's map, until you change it; needs 3D resolution 2x, 4x or 6x; called "Map land" with Squares / Smooth in earlier test builds)
- The game's maps show the land as one colour a square, so coasts, roads and hills are stairs of blocks. On: the land's colours follow the heights between the squares' middles, and the edges between kinds of ground (water, grass, rock, roads) run as smooth curves and diagonals; the colours stay the game's own flat map colours. The squad map, the other side-screen maps and the full map (M). Only the maps' picture changes: the game plays the same.

**Effects pack** (Alt+O, Mods page under Arms pack; Off, the game's own effects, until you change it)
- The game's explosions and other effect animations drawn with the pictures of a mod's `Effects` folder (for example `Effects\1171\01.png`, `02.png` ... in `Mods\Fireball.zip`; 1171 is the fireball of every death). Choose it here or with `screen_effects=` in tnfree.ini; it changes at once, also in the middle of a mission. Animations the pack has no pictures for stay the game's own.
- A pack can have more pictures than the game's animation (30 for the fireball's 15, or any other number): they are spread over the same time, so the explosion runs smoother and still lasts as long as the game's. Pictures at 2 or 3 times the game's size take the same room in the world and look sharp at 3D resolution 4x and 6x. The colours are matched to the game's palette in use (also on planets that change it).
- See-through pixels: a picture's alpha counts. Alpha under 16 is not drawn, 240 and over is solid, and everything in between is mixed with what is behind it by that much (the nearest of the game's colours to the mix, as the eye sees colours), so soft smoke and the edges of a fireball are soft, with no dots. (Earlier test builds drew every pixel from alpha 128 solid.)
- Only the picture changes: the game plays the same (each effect starts and ends as before). It is drawn in the sharp 3D view, so a pack turns that view on even at 3D resolution Original. How to make a pack, the animation numbers and sizes: docs/modding/EFFECT-PACKS.md in the source.
- The fire, sparks and debris the game makes with code can't be changed by a pack.
- The fireball of a death can show other pictures by what died: a pack's folder `1183` for trees and bushes (as tall as the plant was drawn, Tree size too), `1192` for rocks and grassy mounds, `1184` for soldiers (1.25 times as big) and `1167` for vehicles and gun turrets (1.5 times). Without that folder, the fireball's own pictures show as before; buildings and aircraft always keep the fireball. The game's effect is the same: it lasts as long and stands where the fireball does.
- An effect's picture is no longer cut off at the edge of the game's frame when the pack's picture has other proportions.
- The blast marks on scorched ground can be the pack's too (folder `BlastMarks`: 64 x 64 pictures for the squares further away and the distant land, 256 x 256 ones for the near squares, so they are sharp close up). The DeathSplit-test pack has new ones: charred patches with ash in the middle that fade out at the square's edges, so scorched squares next to each other join up. They look the same on every planet. Only the picture changes.

**Objects pack** (Alt+O, Mods page under Effects pack; Off, the game's own trees and rocks, until you change it)
- The game's trees, bushes and rocks drawn with the pictures of a mod's `Objects` folder, one picture per object (for example `Objects\1160\01.png` for the pine, `03.png` for the round bush, `07.png` for the pile of rocks). Choose it here or with `screen_objpack=` in tnfree.ini; it changes at once. Objects the pack has no picture for stay the game's own.
- The pictures keep the game's palette numbers (8-bit indexed PNG), so each planet colours them as it does the game's own trees, and they are shaded, fogged and drawn in the infrared like them. Pictures at 2 or 3 times the game's size take the same room and look sharp at 3D resolution 4x and 6x. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same. How to make a pack, the frame numbers and sizes: docs/modding/OBJECT-PACKS.md in the source.

**Cockpit pack** (Alt+O, Mods page under Mod set; Off, the stretched cockpit, until you change it; needs Widescreen)
- The cockpit and the goggles painted for 16:9 instead of stretched: a mod's `Cockpits` folder holds a picture for each suit, detail level and view (for example `Cockpits\standard-400-cockpit.png`, 426 x 400, and `standard-400-cockpit-2x.png` at twice the pixels, 852 x 800, used when the window shows the screen at twice its rows or more; `-goggles` for the goggles' frame; `-200-` for the 320 X 200 screen). Choose it here or with `screen_cockpit=` in tnfree.ini; it changes at once. A screen the pack has no picture for stays stretched.
- The game's screen stays 1:1 in the middle (not stretched, so round dials are round and the text is the game's), the pack's art goes around it, and the 3D view is drawn as wide as the pack's window. Wherever the game draws over its art (the screens, gauges, text, help boxes) its pixels show; elsewhere the pack's. A window narrower than 16:9 shows the whole picture with bars above and below. The mouse works on the screen as before and in the wide view beside it; over the pack's art beside the screen the buttons do nothing. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same. How to make a pack, and a starter kit made from your own game files: docs/modding/COCKPIT-PACKS.md in the source.

**Tree size** (Alt+O, Detail page under Water effects; Original until you change it)
- Realistic: every tree, bush and rock has a size of its own, from 0.6 to 2 times the game's, so young trees stand next to old ones. The size comes from the object's place on the map, so it is the same every time you play. Only the picture changes: where shots hit, what you bump into and what blocks the view stay the game's, so a big tree can hide an enemy who can still shoot through its branches. With the game's own pictures or an objects pack, in the infrared, the fog and the water's reflection. Drawn in the sharp 3D view, so it turns that view on even at 3D resolution Original.

**Tree explosion** (Alt+O, Detail page under Tree size; Original until you change it; called Burning plants: Off / Explosive in earlier test builds)
- Original (the game's fireball) or Realistic. A tree or bush that dies is drawn on its own picture, in place of the game's fireball: it lights up from the blast's centre outwards (0.18 s), then the crown bursts into spinning pieces of the plant itself (12 to 60 by its size, most of them burning), a spray of needles, chips of wood, bark and leaf in the plant's own colours, burnt leaves thrown high that flutter down and sparks streaking up, with the blast, brown dust rolling out along the ground and a smoke plume (about 3.6 s). The ground is left with the game's blast marks (the pack's when it has them).
- What is left of the plant stays standing until the mission ends, each kind of plant its own (a stump, burnt sticks, wilted stems or nothing); it stands behind the blast, so the blast hides it at first, and its broken top glows and sheds ash for a while. Up to 240 stay at a time, the oldest going first. They are the picture only (they don't stop shots or units) and they are not kept in a saved game.
- The blast, the leftovers and the chips are the Effects pack's pictures (folders PlantBlast, Leftovers and Pieces; the DeathSplit-test pack has them all), the smoke (1175) and the dust (1170) too. A pack with no PlantBlast uses its fireball (1171); with no leftover for a plant its broken trunk chars and falls apart into ash as in earlier test builds; without a pack the game's fireball is the blast. The pieces, the needles, the burnt leaves and the sparks are the plant's own pixels and need no pack. Rocks and grassy mounds don't burn: they keep their death (1192 in a pack, or the fireball).
- It works with the game's trees and with an Objects pack's, at their Tree size, in the fog, at night and in the infrared (the fire is hot there). The land, buildings and nearer objects hide it as they hid the plant. Only the picture changes: the game plays the same (the death, its time and sound, what shots hit). Drawn in the sharp 3D view, so it turns that view on even at 3D resolution Original.
- Flash fire, the second style in the earlier test builds (fire spreading up over the plant), was taken out: with many plants burning it slowed the game too much. A `tnfree.ini` with `screen_burn=flash` or `screen_burn=explosive` gets Realistic.

**Smooth videos** (Alt+O, Sound & movies page; Off until you turn it on)

It needs Vulkan graphics drivers; without them the setting says "not available". About 2 ms a picture on an RTX 5090, 15 to 31 ms on AMD integrated graphics (66 ms is the limit). Your brightness, gamma, contrast and colour settings apply to the new pictures too.

## 0.1.0 test build (2026-10-23)

The first build of Terra Nova: Freefall for testers. It runs Terra Nova: Strike Force Centauri (English v1.09, the Steam and GOG release) natively on Windows, without DOSBox, from a full decompilation of the original program. With every option off it plays exactly like the DOS game: missions 1, 2, 10 and 25 were compared frame by frame with the original and match.

**Runs on modern Windows**
- One `tnfree.exe`, no installer and no extra files. It finds your Steam or GOG game folder (asks once) and reads the maps and movies from the game's CD image.
- Saved games, settings and the log stay next to `tnfree.exe`; nothing goes into your game folder or AppData.
- Resizable window, full screen with Alt+Enter.
- Music as Sound Blaster FM (OPL3), Windows General MIDI, or off.
- Works with Terra Nova Plus installs.

**Optional improvements** (all off until you turn them on, Alt+O)
- **Smooth frame rate**: 60 frames a second. The original's shots stop hitting anything above about 30 frames a second; Freefall fixes the hit test, and rain falls at the right speed.
- **Advanced controls**: mouselook with the reticle at the centre, Align body to headview (F), Gimbal mode (X), mouse wheel zoom, middle button or Left Shift to free the pointer.
- **Auto target camera** on the left screen.
- **Keybindings** for missions, separately for the Vanilla and Advanced controls, including mouse buttons and the wheel.
- **Music packs**: OGG or MP3 files in place of the game's music.
- An options screen inside the game (Alt+O, or the Freefall options button on the game's options screen) with one switch for the original game.

**Fixed**
- 15 bugs of the original game that DOS happened to tolerate but crash a modern system (G1 to G15 in BUGS.txt), including crashes near the map's edge (mission 4), near the base (mission 6), on exit from the options screen, and in AI path planning.
- 12 bugs found in the port itself during testing (P1 to P12).

**Known issues**
- Not digitally signed yet: browsers and SmartScreen warn when you download or start it.
- General MIDI uses Windows' built-in synthesizer, which sounds thin; FM is closer to the original.
- The head-mounted displays of 1996 are not supported.
- Windows only for now.
